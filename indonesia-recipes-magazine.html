Padang Beef Rendang
(Rendang Daging Sapi Minangkabau)

★★★★★ 5 (1420 reviews)

CNN’s World #1 Dish: beef chuck slow-braised in rich coconut milk and aromatic bumbu rempah paste until liquid evaporates and meat caramelizes into dark, tender, intensely spiced perfection.

◉ Prep 35 min ◉ Cook 180 min ◉ Yield 6 serv ◉ 540 kcal

Ingredients

  • 1 kg beef chuck, cut into 4 cm cubes
  • 1 L fresh thick coconut milk & 500 ml thin coconut milk
  • Spice Paste (Bumbu): Shallots, garlic, curly red chilies, ginger, galangal, turmeric, candlenuts
  • Aromatics: 3 bruised lemongrass stalks, 5 kaffir lime leaves, 2 turmeric leaves, 3 Indonesian bay leaves (daun salam)
  • 1 piece asam kandis or tamarind paste, sea salt to taste

Instructions

  1. Blend shallots, garlic, chilies, ginger, galangal, turmeric, and candlenuts into a smooth spice paste.
  2. Combine spice paste, coconut milk, lemongrass, kaffir lime leaves, turmeric leaves, and bay leaves in a wide wok.
  3. Bring to a boil while stirring continuously to prevent coconut milk from splitting.
  4. Add beef chunks, reduce heat to low, and simmer gently for 2 hours until coconut milk turns oily (kalio stage).
  5. Continue cooking on low heat while stirring carefully until liquid is fully absorbed and the spices turn dark mahogany brown.
Continuous Stirring

In the final hour, stir constantly to prevent bottom scorching while spices caramelize.

Chicken Rendang

Use bone-in chicken thighs cooked for 60 minutes.

About Indonesian Cuisine (Nusantara)

The Spice Islands: thousands of islands, ancient bumbu pastes, and caramelized coconut rendang.

Introduction to Indonesian Food (Masakan Nusantara)

Indonesia is the historic Spice Islands of world trade—an archipelago spanning over 17,000 islands and hundreds of ethnic groups, each contributing unique chili sambals, aromatic roots, and heritage cooking techniques.

Indonesian cooking is celebrated for its intense, multi-layered depth: sweet palm sugar, sour tamarind, pungent shrimp paste, creamy coconut milk, and fiery bird’s eye chilies harmonizing in perfect balance.

The Core Pillars of Nusantara Cuisine

Built upon fresh stone-ground spice pastes (Bumbu), fragrant rice accompaniments, and artisanal fermented sambals.

01. The Sacred Bumbu Rempah

Fresh spice pastes ground on stone cobek mortars using shallots, garlic, galangal, turmeric, and ginger.

02. Slow Caramelization (Kalio to Rendang)

Simmering meats in spiced coconut milk until water evaporates and spices fry in pure coconut oil.

03. Sambal as the Soul of the Meal

Over 300 regional chili sambals providing personalized heat, acidity, and umami.

04. Wok Hei & Charcoal Fire

Fierce street wok tossing and coconut shell charcoal grilling that imparts irresistible smoky perfume.

Iconic Dishes of Indonesian Gastronomy

World-renowned masterpieces voted among the most delicious foods on Earth.

Padang Beef Rendang (Rendang Daging Minangkabau)

Beef chuck slow-braised in rich coconut milk, lemongrass, and 12 spices until dark mahogany and caramelized.

Cultural Note: Voted CNN’s World #1 Dish, representing Minangkabau hospitality and philosophy.

Special Fried Rice (Nasi Goreng Spesial Komplit)

Smoky wok-tossed chilled rice with sweet soy sauce (kecap manis), shrimp paste, chicken, and sunny-side-up egg.

Cultural Note: Indonesia’s universal national comfort food eaten from street carts to state banquets.

Madura Chicken Satay (Sate Ayam Bumbu Kacang)

Charcoal-grilled chicken skewers drenched in roasted sweet peanut sauce, kecap manis, and fried shallots.

Cultural Note: The world’s most recognized skewered street meat with rich peanut sauce.

Jakarta Soto Betawi (Soto Betawi Kuah Santan)

Tender beef shank in a creamy coconut and evaporated milk broth scented with nutmeg and lemongrass.

Cultural Note: Jakarta’s indigenous heritage soup topped with bitter-crisp emping melinjo crackers.

Gado-Gado Salad (Gado-Gado Jakarta)

Blanched greens, bean sprouts, fried tofu, tempeh, and boiled egg drenched in rich tamarind peanut dressing.

Cultural Note: Indonesia’s national salad celebrating plant-based richness.

Indonesian Jajanan Pasar & Sweets

Traditional morning market snacks (jajanan pasar) crafted from fragrant pandan juice, glutinous rice flour, fresh coconut, and dark palm sugar.

"Enjoying hot sweet ginger tea (Wedang Jahe) or thick black tubruk coffee alongside warm steamed market cakes on rainy tropical mornings."

Essential Indonesian Pantry Ingredients

🍶 Kecap Manis (Sweet Soy Sauce) (Kecap Manis Tradisional)

Thick, molasses-rich sweet soy sauce infused with palm sugar and star anise.

Use: Stir-fries, satay marinades, fried rice, and dipping sauces.
🫚 Lengkuas (Galangal) (Laos / Lengkuas Segar)

Pungent rhizome with a sharp citrusy pine aroma essential to rendang and soto.

Use: Pounded in spice pastes and bruised in simmering curries.
🍃 Pandan Leaves (Daun Pandan) (Daun Pandan Wangi)

The "vanilla of the East" providing intense floral grassy aroma and natural green color.

Use: Steaming fragrant rice, flavoring coconut milk, and traditional desserts.
🦐 Terasi (Shrimp Paste) (Terasi Udang Fermentasi)

Sun-dried fermented shrimp blocks delivering deep savory umami when toasted.

Use: Fried rice, spicy sambals, and vegetable stir-fries.
🥥 Gula Jawa (Palm Sugar) (Gula Merah Kelapa)

Unrefined dark coconut palm sugar with deep caramel and butterscotch notes.

Use: Sweet peanut sauces, marinades, and traditional desserts.
🌿 Daun Jeruk (Kaffir Lime Leaves) (Daun Jeruk Purut)

Double leaves packed with refreshing, citrusy essential oils.

Use: Torn fresh into rendang, soups, and fried chicken.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Beef Rendang cooked for so many hours?

Slow cooking allows the coconut milk water to evaporate until pure coconut oil separates, naturally caramelizing and preserving the beef.

What is the secret to authentic Nasi Goreng?

Using chilled, day-old dry jasmine rice tossed in a smoking hot wok with sweet kecap manis and toasted shrimp paste.

What makes Indonesian peanut sauce different from others?

Indonesian peanut sauce uses fresh roasted peanuts stone-ground with garlic, bird’s eye chilies, kaffir lime, tamarind, and dark palm sugar.
