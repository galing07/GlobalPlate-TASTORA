Tacos al Pastor
(Tacos al Pastor con Piña Asada)

★★★★★ 5 (1380 reviews)

Thinly sliced pork shoulder marinated in achiote paste, guajillo chiles, and pineapple juice, seared with caramelized pineapple chunks, chopped cilantro, and salsa verde on warm corn tortillas.

◉ Prep 30 min ◉ Cook 20 min ◉ Yield 4 serv ◉ 480 kcal

Ingredients

  • 800 g pork shoulder, sliced paper-thin
  • 3 dried Guajillo chiles & 2 Ancho chiles, stemmed & seeded
  • 50 g Achiote paste & 100 ml pineapple juice
  • 2 tbsp white vinegar, 3 garlic cloves, 1 tsp Mexican oregano
  • 1 fresh ripe pineapple, peeled and sliced
  • 16 small warm corn tortillas, chopped white onion, fresh cilantro, lime wedges

Instructions

  1. Soak dried chiles in hot water 15 mins. Blend with achiote paste, pineapple juice, vinegar, garlic, oregano, and salt.
  2. Marinate thin pork slices in chile paste for at least 4 hours (or overnight).
  3. Sear marinated pork in a screaming-hot cast iron skillet with oil until charred at edges (3 mins/batch).
  4. Grill pineapple slices until caramelized; chop into bite-sized pieces.
  5. Mound crispy pork onto warm double corn tortillas, crown with roasted pineapple, onion, cilantro, and fresh salsa.
High Heat Sear

Cooking in small batches on a smoking cast iron replicates the vertical trompo grill char.

About Mexican Cuisine (Cocina Tradicional)

UNESCO World Heritage: ancient corn nixtamalization, fire-roasted chilies, and complex heirloom moles.

Introduction to Traditional Mexican Cuisine

Recognized by UNESCO in 2010 as an Intangible Cultural Heritage, traditional Mexican cuisine is a comprehensive cultural model spanning ancient Mesoamerican agriculture, nixtamalization of native maize, and celebratory communal rituals.

Far beyond fast-food stereotypes, authentic Mexican cooking is an ancient alchemy of fire-roasted dried chilies, fragrant Mexican oregano, Mexican cinnamon (canela), fresh epazote, and velvety slow-simmered moles.

The Sacred Pillars of Mexican Cooking

Rooted in the Mesoamerican "Milpa" triad—corn, beans, and squash—enhanced with native chilies and wild herbs.

01. Nixtamalization of Maize

Boiling corn kernels in alkaline lime water to unlock nutrients and form pliable masa dough for tortillas.

02. The Holy Trinity of Chilies

Poblano, Guajillo, Ancho, and Pasilla chiles toasted to release smoky, fruity, and raisin notes.

03. Molcajete Stone Grinding

Hand-grinding roasted aromatics in volcanic basalt mortars to extract essential botanical oils.

04. The Complexity of Mole

Layering up to 30 ingredients—seeds, dried fruits, chiles, and Mexican chocolate—into a unified symphony.

Popular Traditional Mexican Dishes

Beloved culinary treasures from the fondas, street taquerías, and family kitchens of Mexico.

Tacos al Pastor (Tacos al Pastor con Piña)

Achiote and guajillo-marinated pork shoulder seared crisp with caramelized pineapple, white onion, and cilantro.

Cultural Note: Mexico City’s iconic street taco born from Lebanese culinary fusion.

Jalisco Quesabirria Tacos (Quesabirria con Consomé)

Braised beef in rich dried chile broth, folded into crispy corn tortillas with melted Oaxaca cheese, dipped in hot consomé.

Cultural Note: Jalisco’s festive stew transformed into the world’s most viral dipped taco.

Puebla Mole Poblano (Mole Poblano con Pollo)

Chicken simmered in a velvety dark sauce of 4 dried chilies, Mexican chocolate, almonds, raisins, and sesame seeds.

Cultural Note: Mexico’s national dish created in 17th-century Puebla convents.

Enchiladas Verdes Suizas (Enchiladas Suizas Gratinadas)

Rolled corn tortillas with shredded chicken, covered in roasted tomatillo-serrano cream salsa and broiled bubbly cheese.

Cultural Note: The ultimate Mexican comfort food comfort bake.

Molcajete Guacamole (Guacamole Tradicional)

Fresh Hass avocados crushed in volcanic stone mortar with serrano chilies, lime, cilantro, and warm corn totopos.

Cultural Note: Ancient Aztec dish celebrated across the globe.

Essential Mexican Ingredients

🌽 Masa Harina (Nixtamalized Corn) (Masa de Maíz Nixtamalizado)

Fine flour made from stone-ground corn treated with calcium hydroxide.

Use: Handmade corn tortillas, tamales, and sopes.
🌶️ Dried Guajillo & Ancho Chiles (Chiles Secos Tradicionales)

Sun-dried chiles with sweet berry notes (Ancho) and bright tangy heat (Guajillo).

Use: Marinades, birria broths, red salsas, and moles.
🍈 Fresh Tomatillos (Tomate Verde / Tomatillo)

Tart green fruits encased in papery husks with refreshing acidity.

Use: Roasted and blended for Salsa Verde and enchilada sauces.
🍫 Mexican Chocolate (Canela) (Chocolate de Mesa con Canela)

Coarsely ground cacao blended with cinnamon, sugar, and almond.

Use: Mole Poblano and frothy hot drinking chocolate.
🧀 Queso Oaxaca (Queso Oaxaca / Quesillo)

Semi-soft stringy white melting cheese from Oaxaca.

Use: Quesabirrias, quesadillas, and melted cheese bakes.
🌿 Fresh Epazote & Cilantro (Hierbas Aromáticas Mexicanas)

Ancient aromatic herbs with pungent earthy and citrus notes.

Use: Simmered in black beans, quesadillas, and fresh salsas.

Mexican Food Traditions & Feasts

Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead)

November 1-2 (Autumn)

Families create vibrant altars (ofrendas) loaded with marigolds, Pan de Muerto sugar bread, and the favorite foods of departed loved ones.

Staples: Pan de Muerto, Mole Negro, Tamales, Atole

Las Posadas & Christmas (Nochebuena)

December (Winter)

Nine nights of community processions celebrating with sweet fruit punch (Ponche Navideño), pork tamales, and buñuelos with piloncillo syrup.

Staples: Tamales de Puerco, Ponche Navideño, Bacalao a la Vizcaína, Buñuelos

Fiestas Patrias (Independence Feast)

September 15-16

Celebrating Mexican independence with patriotic red, white, and green dishes honoring the national flag.

Staples: Chiles en Nogada, Pozole Rojo, Tacos al Pastor, Tequila

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Nixtamalization and why is it essential?

Soaking corn in alkaline water softens the hard outer pericarp, releases vitamin B3 (niacin), and allows it to grind into elastic dough for tortillas.

Is Mexican chocolate only for sweet desserts?

No; in Mole Poblano and savory sauces, dark chocolate adds earthy bitterness and velvety thickness that balances roasted chiles.

What gives Quesabirria its famous crispy orange crust?

Tortillas are dipped directly in the spiced chile fat rendered from the braised beef before frying on the hot flat comal griddle.
